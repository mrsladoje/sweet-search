\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Libraries that compute late-interaction (MaxSim) scores for ColBERT-style reranking do not all compute the same score: they differ in normalization, epsilon placement, clamping and padding.
The rules nearly agree on float vectors but diverge on int4 tokens.
We present \benchname, a benchmark for CPU MaxSim kernels.
It defines each scoring rule as a contract and checks every implementation against a float64 reference of its own rule.
These checks found two new defects in other libraries, among them invalid scores from NumKong on Intel CPUs with AMX, and confirmed a known one.
The benchmark replays rerank calls captured from a code-search engine and from two BEIR datasets, and it keeps development and held-out data apart under a recorded freeze.
We compare an int4 SIMD kernel from the open-source engine sweet-search with NumPy, PyTorch, maxsim-cpu, NextPlaid and NumKong on five CPUs.
With warm calls, the kernel was faster than every frozen exact float32 rival configuration on the captured agent and code-search calls on all five CPUs.
On agent calls, the fastest rival needed \nm{best.warm.agent-ho.f32.min}--\nm{best.warm.agent-ho.f32.max}$\times$ the kernel's time.
We had seen aggregates of these sets before the freeze.
On the fresh BEIR calls, the kernel was faster on four CPUs, and PyTorch was faster on the Xeon.
Rivals were also faster with one thread, with many callers in one process, after a busy gap, on large calls, and with NumKong's approximate scores.
In the engine's own search path, the kernel took a median of \nm{pipe.maxsim.med}\% of an agent search call.
A pre-registered test ($k = 100$, margin 0.01 nDCG@10) found the int4 scoring rule non-inferior to float32 for ColBERTv2 on SciFact and NFCorpus.
For answerai-colbert-small, it lost up to \nm{q.intel.nfcorpus.answersmall.relabs}\%.

\end{abstract}
