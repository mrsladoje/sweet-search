\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Libraries that compute MaxSim do not compute one function, so a benchmark must check each implementation against its own contract before it times it.
With such checks, \benchname found two new defects and confirmed a known one in public libraries.
It also separated the effect of a contract from the effect of an approximation.
The contracts nearly agree on float vectors and diverge once tokens are quantized.
On five CPUs, the int4 kernel of sweet-search was faster than the exact float32 rivals on warm agent and code-search calls.
It was not faster with one thread, after a busy gap, with many callers in one process, or on some large calls.
In the search path of sweet-search, the kernel took a median of \nm{pipe.maxsim.med}\% of an agent search call, so its speed changed little there.
Its int4 rule kept the quality of ColBERTv2 and lost quality with answerai-colbert-small, a model whose candidates score close together.
We release the harness, the replay format, the references and every raw result, so that others can add implementations, models and CPUs.
