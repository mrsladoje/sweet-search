Verdict first: Slate A ships almost nothing. Of nine core candidates (C-1 through C-9) plus one
reserve (R-1), only **C-1** (the read gutter) survived to production. **C-4** (whole-file
expansion) was built, shipped, then reversed by its own live measurement — cost rose on all three
harnesses instead of falling. Everything else died at a `$0` gate. The two largest dollar movements
in the whole program were **accounting bugs**, not product levers: a sidechain-pricing
de-duplication defect (~10 points) and a run-directory-name transcription tax (~7.4 points). The
draft register (`DEAD-LEVER-REGISTER-DRAFT.md`) is mostly accurate for what it covers, but it is
missing several rows entirely, has one row that is stale-wrong (D-4), one row whose source citation
does not exist (C-4/B12), and one row that quietly conflates two different measurement runs (C-5/E5).
