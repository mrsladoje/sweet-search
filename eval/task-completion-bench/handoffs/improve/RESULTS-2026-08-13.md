**Cache-normalized versus realized turns out not to matter**: −25.46% against −24.18% on
the same complete rows, a 1.3-point difference. The earlier alarm about that choice was an
artifact of the null-driven bias above.

**The remaining spread is one decision, not four.** With the tax settled, everything hinges

the rule so that decision can never again be made after seeing the result.

| tool calls per rollout, as measured | 35.7 | 19.9 | −44.3% |

Paired bootstrap on per-task means, 20,000 resamples: the headline interval is roughly
**[−40%, +22%], p ≈ 0.34**; uncorrected it was CI [−46.4%, +6.0%], p = 0.099.
**Nothing here reaches significance, and the sensitivity is further from it than the
headline.** Per-task standard error is **18.8 points** against a −19.51% effect.

**Why the sensitivity is so much smaller.** Four of the five degenerate rollouts are native.
Removing them takes far more spend out of native's column than out of sweet's. Sweet's
advantage therefore rests partly on native having drawn four decoding blow-ups in 32
rollouts and sweet only one. That is a sampling accident, not a property of either tool —
which is exactly why §9 pre-registers a retry rule rather than an exclusion rule.

**The project goal — cheaper AND solves more, on all three harnesses — is NOT met.**
This is cheaper, level on solve, on one harness, without significance.

## 1. The `pages` harness tax, and why native's cost had to be corrected

The routed backbone (`gpt-5.6-luna` through Claude Code's Anthropic skin) emits an invalid
`pages` argument on ordinary source reads. Claude Code rejects it before reading the file.

**Two invalid spellings occur, not one** (census of all 299 native Read calls):

| `pages` value sent | calls | rejected |
|---|---:|---:|
| `"1"` | 174 | 0 |
| `""` | **99** | **99** |
| `" "` (single space) | **11** | **11** |
| `"1-10"` / `"1-5"` / `"1-20"` | 15 | 0 |

**Correction, 2026-08-13.** An earlier version of this section said the model "eats several
rejections and switches to `pages: "1"`, so the tax is bounded and front-loaded". The first
half is wrong. Of the 32 native sessions, **only 10 stop sending the invalid value after their
first rejection; 22 keep sending it**. The tax is front-loaded (median rejection is the 2nd
Read call) and bounded in size (3.4 rejections per session), but it is *not* a one-time
lesson the model learns.

Measured from the per-request transcripts of all 64 rollouts:

| arm | sessions | wasted Read calls | cost of the wasted round trips |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| native | 32 | **110** | **`$0.035160`** |
| sweet | 32 | **0** | **`$0.000000`** |

**Settled 2026-08-13.** Each rejection costs exactly **one extra assistant round trip**. Two
independent ways of pricing that round trip agree:

| attribution | total | verdict |
|---|---:|---|
| the turn that issued the rejected call | `$0.035160` | **adopted** |
| the turn that retried it | `$0.035097` | agrees to **0.2%** — corroboration |
| both turns | `$0.070257` | double-counts one round trip |
| every turn until a Read succeeds | `$0.221819` | charges unrelated work |
| ~~previously published~~ | ~~`$0.054326`~~ | **retired** — matches no rule |

The two admissible attributions landing within 0.2% of each other is the evidence: the
issuing turn and the retry turn cost the same, so the marginal cost of the extra trip is
unambiguous. The figure carried in the first version of this document, `$0.054326`, sits
between "one turn" and "two turns" and cannot be derived from either.

The defect hits **all 16 native tasks** — systematic, not concentrated — and costs
**7.4% of native's total**.

**Why sweet pays nothing:** it barely uses Claude Code's `Read` tool at all (58 calls
against native's 499), because it reads through `ss-read`. So sweet does not avoid the
bug by being better; it avoids it by not touching the broken tool.

**Why this is a harness artifact, not a native weakness.** A native arm driven by an
actual Claude model would not emit `pages: ""`. The tax is a property of running a
non-Claude backbone through Claude Code, so leaving it in native's column would credit
sweet for a defect in our adapter. It is subtracted for the headline and disclosed here.

**Residual limit.** Context growth compounds slightly across a session, so removing a
mid-session round trip is not perfectly equivalent to it never happening. The effect is
second-order and bounded by the 0.2% agreement between the two attributions above.

**No mitigation is possible at the hook layer.** This was believed to be partly fixed. It is
not fixed at all — see §5.

## 5. The `pages` mitigation: the hook layer cannot work, and never worked

**Superseded 2026-08-13.** This section previously reported a partial mitigation and an
"activation race". Both were wrong. A full correlation of hook invocations against Read
outcomes, across all 32 native sessions, settles it.

### 5.1 The measurement

| | Read calls | needed normalizing | rejected |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| hook **ran** | 189 | **0** | **0** |
| hook **did not run** | 110 | **110** | **110** |

**That is complete separation.** The hook ran on every call that did not need it, and on
none of the 110 calls that did. Where it ran it was flawless: 189 of 189 `hook_success`,
exit code 0, correct rewrite, ~30 ms. It has **never once repaired a bad input.**

### 5.2 The cause

Claude Code validates a tool's arguments against its schema **before** the `PreToolUse`
stage. `pages: ""` and `pages: " "` both fail that check, so the call dies before any hook
can observe it. **A `PreToolUse` hook can never fix this defect.** The layer is
architecturally wrong, not misconfigured.

### 5.3 Three earlier claims withdrawn

1. **"33.9% hook coverage, an activation race."** There is no race. The real split is 100%
   of schema-valid calls and 0% of invalid ones. The old figure also used a wrong
   denominator (557 rather than 299 native main-transcript Read calls).
2. **"Where it fires it is exact — 3 hooked reads gave 0 errors, 4 unhooked gave 4 errors."**
   That is the separation artifact restated. It is not evidence the hook repairs anything.
3. **"`--settings` was refuted."** The comparison was void: both sides of it measured a
   quantity that was 0. `--settings` was never the variable. Aborting that run at `$0.051`
   was still correct; the *reason* recorded for it was not.

**Process failure, recorded.** The `--settings` change was deployed with no Gate-0 exposure
proof, judged on one run, and written off — the exact anti-pattern the micro-smoke protocol
exists to prevent. The correct diagnosis cost `$0` and twenty minutes of trace correlation.

### 5.4 Why nothing new is being built

The only layer upstream of Claude Code's validator is the network transport. The jail's
egress control (`harness/egress-guard.mjs`) is an SNI-inspecting TCP proxy that
**deliberately never terminates TLS** — no MITM certificate authority exists inside the
jail. Rewriting model replies there would mean breaking TLS end-to-end.

That property closed the hole in which **13 of native's 16 discordant wins were
ground-truth-assisted**. It is not traded for a cost correction that is already measurable
directly from the transcripts.

**Current status: unmitigable at this layer.** The subtraction in §1 is driven by real
rejection counts, so it does not depend on the hook and does not move — though its dollar
attribution carries its own 55% spread, stated there. The hook remains installed,
arm-symmetric and inert; it is now documented as inert so no future session re-derives an
"activation race" from it.

## 6. Threats to validity, stated plainly

1. **Not an old-vs-new design.** This compares sweet against native under the new code. It
   **cannot attribute** the cost gap to the seven fixes. They may account for all, some or
   none of it.
2. **Not comparable to the published 2026-08-11 scoreboard** (`+2.38%`). Four things
   changed together: the product fixes, the cache-write premium, transcript-superset
   pricing, and the removal of `dotnet__yarp-2825`. The swing is suggestive and
   **not attributable**. The internal comparison within this run is clean because both
   arms are priced identically under one build.
3. **n = 16, p ≈ 0.34.** The cost column cannot resolve a difference at this scale. It
   could not at n = 17 either.
   **3a. One accounting choice still moves the result by 15.6 points.** Pricing versus
   removing the degenerate rollouts gives −19.51% or −3.96%. §9 fixes the rule for future
   runs (retry), but this run cannot be retried, so both numbers must travel together.
   **3b. Variance is dominated by decoding blow-ups, not by tasks.** Five rollouts in 64
   carry ratios of 6.95 to 37.74 between billed and retained output. Which arm draws them
   is chance. Any future cost claim must state whether they are in or out.
4. **Main-only pricing.** Claude Code's subagent transcripts are partly usage-zeroed
   (6 of 13 and 3 of 11 messages in the two audited files), so delegated spend cannot be
   fully priced. Main-only excludes more from native (12 delegating rollouts) than from
   sweet (4), which makes it **conservative for sweet**. Adding the recoverable delegated
   floor moves the uncorrected gap from −25.46% to −29.75%.
5. **The screen predates the multi-scope repair**, which cost sweet four zero-return
   `ss-grep` calls on `pytask`. Sweet's figure is therefore slightly pessimistic.
6. **One task carries the tail.** `mransan__ocaml-protoc-202` (−82%) is the zero-character
   issue task, where refusing is correct and cheap. It flatters whichever arm refuses.

## 9. Pre-registration of the cost definition (2026-08-13)

**Binding on every future cost claim from this benchmark.** It exists because this run's
headline moved from −15.85% to −25.46% to −19.51% without a single new rollout, purely by
re-choosing how to count. Each choice below is fixed *before* the next run, with its reason.

### 9.1 Cost column — `idealCostMainOnlyUsd`

Cache-normalized, main-transcript only.

**Why main-only.** It is the only column that is non-null on **32 of 32 rollouts in both
arms**. The sidechain-inclusive columns are null wherever a delegated transcript is
usage-zeroed, which is **12 of 32 native rollouts and 4 of 32 sweet**. Summing a column with
that null pattern silently drops most of native's delegating — and most expensive —
rollouts. **A cost comparison may never be computed over a column that is null at different
rates in the two arms.**

**Why cache-normalized rather than realized.** Convention, and it is nearly free: on the
complete rows the two give −25.46% and −24.18%, a 1.3-point difference. Realized is reported
alongside as a robustness line, never as the headline.

**What this costs us.** Main-only under-charges delegating rollouts, and native delegates
three times as often as sweet (12 against 4). The headline is therefore **conservative for
native and generous to sweet**, and must be labelled so. Restoring the sidechain column is
the highest-value accounting work outstanding.

### 9.2 The `pages` harness tax — removed at `$0.035160`, one round trip per rejection

Settled in §1. Removed from native only, because sweet incurs zero. It is **our adapter's
defect, not native's behaviour**, and leaving it in would credit sweet for our bug.

**Reported both ways, always.** Any published figure states the corrected number and the
uncorrected number, because the correction is ours to make and a reader is entitled to check
it.

### 9.3 Degenerate rollouts — **retried, never priced and never excluded**

A rollout that trips the arm-blind detector in `harness/degeneration.mjs` is **re-run**, and
the retry replaces it. This is the one genuinely open decision, and it is fixed here.

**Why not price them.** A decoding blow-up is a malfunction of the decoder, not a property
of the retrieval tool under test. Pricing it makes the scoreboard partly a lottery on which
arm draws blow-ups — and in this run that lottery is worth **15.6 percentage points**
(−19.51% against −3.96%) on a 4-to-1 native-to-sweet split.

**Why not exclude them.** Excluding after seeing the split is the exact researcher degree of
freedom this section exists to remove. It also quietly rewards whichever arm got lucky, and
it discards a rollout that may have been a solve.

**Why retry.** It is arm-blind, decided by a detector that never sees the arm, cheap
(5 in 64 ≈ 8% more rollouts), and it yields a complete sample rather than a censored one.

**For this run, which cannot be retried retrospectively:** the headline prices every
rollout, and the exclusion is published beside it as a declared sensitivity. Both numbers
travel together or neither travels.

### 9.4 Standing rules

1. **No cost figure is published as a single number** without its sensitivity beside it.
2. **No column is summed across arms until its null rate is checked in both arms.**
3. **Solve counts and tool-call counts are unaffected by all of the above** and remain the
   claims that need no accounting caveat.
4. **Any change to this section is dated and justified in place.** The three superseded
   figures — `$0.054326`, −15.85%, and the four-view range — stay visible in §0 and §1
   rather than being deleted.
