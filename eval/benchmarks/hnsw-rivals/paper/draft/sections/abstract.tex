\begin{abstract}
We compare candidate generators for single-query dense retrieval on CPUs at a fixed fraction of the
task quality of exact search, not at a recall target. Seven sets (code search and BEIR, 7k to 1M
vectors), three 768-dimensional embedding models and five CPUs (Intel Granite Rapids, AMD Zen 5, AWS
Graviton4, AMD Zen 3, Apple M3 Max) cover a binary HNSW cascade with an int8 screen and float16
rescoring, and FAISS, USearch, hnswlib, ScaNN, DiskANN and Intel SVS indexes. Operating points are
chosen on dev queries and frozen before a single held-out run. Under a common rescoring procedure
(comparison A), the binary scan or binary HNSW walk of the cascade is the fastest generator that keeps
99\% of exact quality in all \nm{A-oursbest} CPU--set cells, \nm{A-min} to \nm{A-max} times faster than
the next; partition generators are faster at their smallest declared effort but lose up to 77\% of
the quality on the larger sets. As shipped (comparison B, 99\% of exact quality), the cascade is faster on the 0.5M--1M
sets (geometric mean ratio \nm{gm-large}, ours faster in \nm{faster-large} of \nm{n-large} cells),
mixed in between, and mostly slower on code sets below 22k vectors, where FAISS binary flat wins
(geometric mean \nm{gm-small}). Quality non-inferiority at $\delta = \nm{delta}$ holds in
\nm{nprimarypass} of \nm{nprimary} primary comparisons (Holm). The cascade uses more memory than most
rivals at 1M, builds single-threaded for \nm{build-ours-h} hours, and its memory-mapped index is
sensitive to page-cache pressure. We release the harness, the frozen points and every held-out record.
\end{abstract}
