\begin{abstract}
Approximate nearest-neighbour indexes are usually compared at a recall target, but applications see the task
quality of the final ranking. We compare a binary cascade (512-bit sign codes, an HNSW walk or an exact scan, an int8
screen and float16 rescoring) with indexes from FAISS, USearch, hnswlib, ScaNN, DiskANN and Intel SVS. Each system
runs at the fastest configuration that keeps 99\% of the task quality of exact search, chosen on dev queries and
frozen before any held-out run. An exploratory main study covers seven code and BEIR sets (7k to 1M vectors), three
embedding models and five CPUs. It gives three findings. First, recall is a poor proxy for task quality: \nm{rec-dist-low}
of our \nm{rec-dist-n} distinct operating points have recall@10 between \nm{rec-dist-min} and 0.9 and still keep 99\% of
exact quality on held-out queries. Second, on the same
512-bit codes at equal graph settings, our HNSW walk generates candidates \nm{post-eqb-min}--\nm{post-eqb-max} times
faster than the binary HNSW of FAISS and USearch. Third, in a pre-registered replication on two untouched sets
(CQADupStack, 457k documents; MS MARCO, 1M passages) on three cloud CPUs, the cascade was faster than the rival chosen on
dev in all \nm{rep-n} tests (\nm{rep-min}--\nm{rep-max} times, Holm-corrected). Quality non-inferiority held on MS MARCO
only. The cascade loses below about 20k vectors, uses more memory, builds slowly and is sensitive to page-cache
eviction. We release the harness, the frozen configurations and every record.
\end{abstract}
