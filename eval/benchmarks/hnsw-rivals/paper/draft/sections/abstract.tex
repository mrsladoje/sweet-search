\begin{abstract}
In a pre-registered replication on two untouched sets (CQADupStack, 457k documents; MS MARCO, 1M passages) with a
corrected harness, our binary cascade was faster than the rival chosen on dev in all \nm{rep-n} set--CPU tests (latency
ratio \nm{rep-min}--\nm{rep-max}, Holm-corrected), and quality non-inferiority at 0.5\% of exact quality held on MS
MARCO on all three CPUs but not on CQADupStack. The main study behind it is descriptive. We compare candidate generators for single-query dense retrieval on CPUs at a fixed fraction of the task
quality of exact search, not at a recall target. Seven sets (code search and BEIR, 7k to 1M vectors),
three 768-dimensional embedding models and five CPUs (Intel Granite Rapids, AMD Zen 5, AWS Graviton4,
AMD Zen 3, Apple M3 Max) cover a binary HNSW cascade with an int8 screen and float16 rescoring, and
indexes from FAISS, USearch, hnswlib, ScaNN, DiskANN and Intel SVS. Operating points are chosen on dev
queries and frozen before a single held-out run. Under a common int8 screen and float16 rescoring
(comparison A), whenever one of the cascade's binary generators keeps 99\% of exact quality it is the
fastest generator that does, over every CPU, set, model and budget; part of that margin is the cascade's
faster implementation of the shared steps, but post-freeze runs with per-stage clocks and equal graph
settings confirm it for the generator alone (rival graph generators \nm{post-eq-min}--\nm{post-eq-max} times
slower, geometric mean \nm{post-eq-gm}). At small candidate budgets, float32 graphs keep 99\% in some cells
where the binary codes do not. As shipped (comparison B), against the
rival that was fastest on dev at 99\% of exact quality, the cascade is faster on the 0.5M--1M sets in
\nm{faster-large} of \nm{n-large} cells (geometric mean ratio \nm{gm-large}), mixed in between, and slower
on most code sets below 22k vectors, where an exact binary scan in FAISS wins (geometric mean
\nm{gm-small}). Quality non-inferiority at $\delta = \nm{delta}$ holds in \nm{nprimarypass} of
\nm{nconfirm} confirmatory comparisons (Holm). The cascade uses more memory than most rivals at 1M,
builds single-threaded for \nm{build-ours-h} hours, has a longer latency tail, and its memory-mapped files
cause slow rounds when other I/O evicts them from the page cache, which no rival showed. Code-set results are exploratory, because our defaults were tuned earlier on the same code queries; the
BEIR comparisons are the confirmatory part. Deviations from the pre-registered plan are listed. We
release the harness, the frozen points and every held-out record.
\end{abstract}
