\section{Where the cascade loses}\label{sec:losses}

The losses bound what the results support, so we list them here and not only in the appendix.

\paragraph{Small corpora.} On the small code sets (7k--22k vectors) the rival fastest on dev is faster
than our cascade in \nm{slower-small} of \nm{n-small} cells at $\fr = 0.99$, with ratios down to
\nm{min-small}. That rival is usually FAISS binary flat, which does the same first step as our cascade
on these sets (an exact Hamming scan of 512-bit codes) and then refines in float. At this size the scan
is cheap, so the fixed per-query cost of the rest of the pipeline decides. Our timed call adds an int8
stage, JavaScript orchestration and metadata reads (\S\ref{sec:timing}); which of these dominates is not
established.

\paragraph{Specific rivals at scale.} On Quora with bge-base-en-v1.5, SVS Vamana with LeanVec on Intel and
FAISS binary HNSW on Graviton are faster than ours at $\fr = 0.99$. SVS (LVQ or LeanVec) is also faster on
FiQA on Intel. SVS runs on Intel only in this study.

\paragraph{Small candidate budgets.} In comparison (A), our 512-bit codes need a larger budget than the
float32 graphs on some set--model pairs: in \nm{Aonly-small} of the \nm{Atot-small} cells with
$C \le 200$, only rival generators keep 99\% of exact quality, mostly code sets with bge-base-en-v1.5
and FiQA with CodeRankEmbed. The sign code of the first 512 dimensions keeps less of the ranking for
these model--set pairs.

\paragraph{Quality on FiQA.} With CodeRankEmbed our cascade never reached 99\% of exact quality on FiQA
dev, so it has no operating point there at that fraction. With bge-base-en-v1.5 the non-inferiority test
fails on Intel, and the two other FiQA--bge pairs had too little power to be confirmatory. FiQA has 259
held-out queries; the data do not show a loss larger than $\delta$, but they do not exclude one.

\paragraph{Memory and build.} At 1M vectors our process holds more memory than \nm{mem-smaller-intel} of
the \nm{mem-nriv-intel} rival points on Intel. It keeps the graph with the codes and int8 rows (1.2~GB of
index file at 1M) and the float16 vectors (1.5~GB), plus the Node.js runtime. Our index build is
single-threaded and took \nm{build-ours-h}~hours of wall time with a \nm{build-ours-peak}~GB peak at 1M;
every rival build took less wall time on 16 threads.

\paragraph{Tail latency.} At the 99th percentile the rival fastest on dev is faster than ours in most
pairs (geometric mean ratio \nm{tail-p99}, against \nm{tail-p50} at the median); the source of our longer
tail is not established; the JavaScript part of our timed call (about a third of it, \S\ref{sec:post}) and
the page cache are candidates.

\paragraph{Slow rounds.} Our index is memory-mapped. Counting a round as slow when its median exceeds
three times the fastest round of the same point, \nm{slow-ours} of our \nm{rounds-ours} timed rounds
(\nm{slow-ours-pct}\%) are slow, all on Quora and csn1m, some by a factor of several hundred; none of the
\nm{rounds-riv} rival rounds is. The rivals read their indexes into anonymous memory. The slow rounds
cluster on a few machine instances. The post-freeze runs (\S\ref{sec:post}) show the cause: each slow
round had thousands of major page faults, so pages of our memory-mapped files had been evicted after the
pre-read and were read back from disk during the timed pass. The median over rounds absorbs a single slow round, but in
\nm{slow-moved} of our points (all csn1m, in comparison A) at least half of the rounds are slow, and the
reported median is then a slow value. Locking the index in memory, or reading it into anonymous memory,
would remove the effect at the cost of a longer load.
