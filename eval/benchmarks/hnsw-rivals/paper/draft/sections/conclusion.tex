\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Held to a fixed fraction of the task quality of exact search, a binary cascade answered single queries on 0.5M--1M
vectors faster than the rival chosen on dev, and this held in a pre-registered replication on two untouched sets:
\nm{rep-lat-pass} of \nm{rep-n} latency tests passed on three cloud CPUs (\nm{rep-min}--\nm{rep-max} times). Quality
non-inferiority to that rival held on MS MARCO and not on CQADupStack; on MS MARCO our point also kept 99\% of exact
quality by the bound, on CQADupStack only by the point estimate. One of the six speed passes depended on the
pre-registered page-fault rule; with the index preloaded, all six pass without it. In the exploratory main study, an exact binary scan in FAISS was faster on code sets
below about 20k vectors, float32 graphs kept 99\% of exact quality at smaller candidate budgets for some model--set
pairs, and the cascade's costs were memory, a slow single-threaded build and page-cache misses of its memory-mapped
files. On the same codes and graph settings our binary walk was \nm{post-eqb-min}--\nm{post-eqb-max} times faster than
FAISS and USearch binary HNSW; it computes as many distances, with fewer instructions and branch misses per distance; with common SIMD kernels in the
shared stages the fastest rival generator still took \nm{st-next-min}--\nm{st-next-max} times as long. Post hoc, 768-bit codes kept 99\% of exact
quality at small budgets in more cells than our 512-bit codes, which makes them the first change to test. Recall was a poor guide: operating points with recall@10 as low as \nm{rec-dist-min} kept
99\% of exact task quality. These results hold for the tested sets, models and CPUs; the released records let others
check them and add systems.
