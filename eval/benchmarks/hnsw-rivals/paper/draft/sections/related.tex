\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{ANN benchmarks.} ANN-Benchmarks~\cite{aumuller2020annbenchmarks} and the Big-ANN
competitions~\cite{simhadri2022bigann21,simhadri2024bigann23} compare libraries by recall against exact
neighbours and by throughput, at up to a billion vectors. Experimental studies of graph
indexes~\cite{wang2021graphsurvey,azizi2025graphvector} compare many graph methods on vector
datasets with the same recall metric. Our sets are much smaller (7k to 1M vectors) and our metric is
different: the task quality of the final document ranking, as a fraction of exact search.

\paragraph{Task-centric evaluation.} Iceberg~\cite{chen2026iceberg} argues that recall against exact
neighbours can mislead and follows the loss from embedding to the end task. Recall What
Matters~\cite{dimitropoulos2026recall} argues that Recall@$k$ forces more computation than the task
needs and proposes a distance-based alternative without task labels. We share the starting point; we
measure with the task's own labels and put the generators behind one shared rescoring step.

\paragraph{HNSW against flat search.} Lin~\cite{lin2025operational} compares HNSW and flat indexes,
with and without quantisation, inside Lucene on BEIR, and reports indexing time, query speed and
quality; earlier work argued that one general engine is enough for dense
retrieval~\cite{xian2024lucene}. We compare many libraries and a binary cascade, on code and BEIR sets,
at matched retained quality. Bang for the Buck~\cite{kuffo2025bang} compares vector search on cloud
CPUs for cost and speed at fixed recall; our five-CPU single-thread latency is a narrower, controlled
form of the same hardware question.

\paragraph{Indexes and quantisation.} HNSW~\cite{malkov2020hnsw} is the base graph of our cascade
and of FAISS HNSW, hnswlib and USearch. FAISS~\cite{johnson2021faissgpu,douze2024faiss} provides
binary, scalar-quantised, product-quantised~\cite{jegou2011pq} and RaBitQ~\cite{gao2024rabitq,gao2025extrabitq}
indexes. ScaNN~\cite{guo2020scann} uses anisotropic quantisation; SOAR~\cite{sun2023soar} adds spilled
assignments. DiskANN~\cite{subramanya2019diskann} builds the Vamana graph; SVS adds LVQ and LeanVec
compression to it~\cite{aguerrebere2023lvq,tepper2024leanvec}. Search-time methods such as
CRouting~\cite{li2025crouting}, Ada-ef~\cite{zhang2026adaef}, DARTH~\cite{chatzakis2025darth} and
VSAG~\cite{zhong2025vsag} improve the recall--speed curve of graph search; none of them targets a
task-quality fraction, and we do not test them. Binary codes followed by a full-vector rerank
appear in BPR~\cite{yamada2021bpr}, which learns the hash; our cascade quantises an existing
embedding by sign and adds an int8 screen between the two stages.

\paragraph{Methodology.} We follow the advice on reporting performance
experiments~\cite{hoefler2015scientific} and on measurement bias~\cite{mytkowicz2009wrongdata}, and the
cloud-measurement guidance of~\cite{leitner2016patterns,uta2020bigdata}: repeated rounds on several
instances, A/A runs to size $R$, and intervals for every ratio.

This study differs from the work above in what it holds fixed and what it measures: every generator
feeds one shared rescoring procedure, quality is the task metric of the final document ranking as a
fraction of exact search, with cluster-bootstrap bounds, and latency is single-query on five CPUs.
